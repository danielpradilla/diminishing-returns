A Gallery of Diminishing Returns

When is good good enough?

More can help. But the next hour, dollar, or unit of effort often buys less than the last. These studies show where gains shrink, where they flatten, and why deciding when to stop depends on what you value.

Ten entries · 1967–2025 · Charts rebuilt from data, coefficients, equations and published figures

Method

How to read the charts

Each chart answers one question. The values come from a published dataset, a published equation or fitted coefficient, or a figure that has been digitized. The label on every source record says which. Nothing here is a screenshot, and no data points, confidence bands or optimal thresholds have been invented.

Four different claims

Diminishing gains
The outcome keeps improving, but each equal increment of input produces less improvement.
Plateau
Additional improvement is small or hard to distinguish from uncertainty.
Reversal
Additional input worsens the measured outcome.
Cost-dependent optimum
The stopping point depends on prices, costs, goals or alternatives, not on the curve alone.

Reconstruction labels

Published data
Values come from the authors' data file or published table. Any transformation is stated.
Published coefficients / equation
The curve is computed from a fitted model or formula printed in the paper.
Digitized figure
Coordinates were read from the published figure at high resolution. These values are approximate, and the source record gives the reading error.

A bend is not an optimum

A curve that visibly flattens does not, by itself, establish where anyone should stop. An optimum requires a comparison of marginal gains with marginal costs, and those costs differ across people and settings. For several entries the honest answer is no single stopping point established.

Research productivity over time, theoretical bottlenecks and experimental comparisons are related examples, not interchangeable estimates of one marginal production function. Each is explained on its own terms.

Uncertainty and limits

Confidence intervals or standard errors are preserved where the source reports them. Digitized bands are approximate, and overlap in the original figure can make one band's limits uncertain; this is stated in the entry. Where the original does not report uncertainty, none is drawn.

The page loads only local files. Publisher sites are not contacted at runtime. Sources were checked on 26 October 2026, and the version used for each figure is recorded below.
